How London fares actually work

No jargon — just the bits that affect your wallet.

Daily capping

On contactless or Oyster, there's a maximum you'll pay per day — no matter how many trips. Once you hit it, extra journeys are free. The cap depends on which zones you travel through.

Weekly capping

Contactless only (not Oyster) automatically caps your spending Mon-Sun. This matches the cost of a weekly travel pass — but you don't need to buy one. If you travel less, you pay less.

Peak vs Off-Peak

Peak = Mon-Fri 6:30-9:30am and 4:00-7:00pm. Off-peak is everything else (including weekends and bank holidays). Off-peak fares are 15-35% cheaper.

Boundary stations

Some stations sit on two zones (e.g. Stratford is Zone 2/3). TfL automatically charges you whichever zone gives the cheapest fare. You don't need to do anything.

Bus capping

Buses have a separate daily cap of £5.25 and weekly cap of £24.70. But the contactless weekly cap includes bus travel — once you hit the zone-based weekly cap, buses are free.

Railcard on Oyster

Link a railcard to your Oyster (not contactless) for 1/3 off off-peak fares and reduced daily caps. But Oyster has no weekly capping — so it only wins if you travel off-peak.
